What a decoder actually is
— in maths, and what it costs

An encoder compresses; a decoder goes back. The interesting part is never the direction — it's what information survived the compression, and how much compute you pay to fake what didn't. That's the whole page.

vrinda  ·  salujavrinda018@gmail.com maths first, compute as a budget
00 · the whole idea in one line

Encoder kills information. Decoder can only spend compute trying to get it back.

Everything below is a consequence of two facts: (1) the encoder is a many-to-one map, so information is genuinely destroyed — not hidden; and (2) compute is finite, so you cannot brute-force it back.

x ──E (encoder)──► z ──D (decoder)──► x̂ E is many-to-one: E(x₁) = E(x₂) = z even when x₁ ≠ x₂ ⇒ the exact x is NOT recoverable from z. Ever. No model size fixes this. ⇒ a decoder is a conditional distribution p(x | z), not a function x = D(z).

Hold on to that last line. If you remember one thing from this page: a good decoder is a probability distribution, not a function. That single choice is why the paper you were reading uses a diffusion model.

Frame skip = 4 keeps 1 frame, deletes 3

I( x_{4i+k} ; zᵢ , zᵢ₊₁ ) ≈ 0 for k = 1,2,3 mutual information zᵢ = E(x_{4i}) zᵢ₊₁ = E(x_{4i+4}) the frames x_{4i+1}, x_{4i+2}, x_{4i+3} are in NO z. Their info is gone. ⇒ asking the decoder to output them is asking it to invent, not to recover.

In information terms: the encoder's output rate is roughly d · 5 units/s, while the Cosmos stream carries m · 20. If m·20 > d·5, the decoder must generate the shortfall. There is no way around it.

03 · why “just use a normal decoder” fails

Least-squares training gives you the average — and the average is blur

Train any deterministic decoder f to minimize squared error. The optimum is the conditional mean, and that mean is the weighted blur of every frame that could plausibly sit between the two you observed.

minimize over f : 𝔼 ‖ x − f(z) ‖² set derivative to 0 : f*(z) = 𝔼[ x | z ] ← the conditional mean = a weighted average of ALL possible in-between frames = a smear. A generative decoder instead SAMPLES x ~ p(x | z) → sharp, one plausible world.

04 · the diffusion decoder, mathematically

Learn to undo noise, conditioned on the features

Forward process: destroy x⁰ with Gaussian noise. No learning here — pure arithmetic.

forward (fixed): x^τ = √ᾱ_τ · x⁰ + √(1 − ᾱ_τ) · ε , ε ~ N(0, I) , τ = 0…N reverse (learned): network ε_θ conditioned on z L(θ) = 𝔼 ‖ ε − ε_θ( √ᾱ_τ x⁰ + √(1−ᾱ_τ) ε , τ , z ) ‖² └─ noisy input ─┘ └τ┘ └conditioning┘

“Spatially conditioned” means z is injected patch-aligned: each output patch looks only at the feature at the same grid cell, so conditioning is a local function of space, not one global vector. That's what makes the imagined motion consistent across the frame instead of hallucinated independently per pixel.

τ = N (pure noise)
each of the N steps is one full network forward pass → that's the compute bill.
05 · compute as a budget — drag these

The decoder is ~10⁸× more expensive per step than a linear readout

This is the part that matters for your thesis. A transformer forward pass costs about 2·P·n FLOPs (P params, n tokens). Diffusion needs N of them per output step, and you need 5 output steps per second of 20 fps video.

Where the cost actually lives

  • N multiplies everything. Halving steps halves FLOPs and latency. This is why distillation / few-step samplers are a whole research field.
  • Latency is sequential. The N steps cannot be parallelised across time, so wall-clock scales with N. FLOPs you can buy with GPUs; latency you cannot.
  • Memory is O(P) weights + per-step activations, versus O(d²) for a linear readout. For d = 10³ that's ~2 MB versus gigabytes.
one decoder call: FLOPs ≈ 2 · P · n · N one second of 20 fps video: FLOPs ≈ 5 · 2 · P · n · N = 10 P n N linear readout, one step: FLOPs = 2 d² ratio ≈ (10 P n N) / (2 d²) → drag the sliders and read it off
06 · the trap

A pretty decoded video is not evidence of a good world model

world model predicts: p̂( z_{t+1} | z_t ) lives in feature space decoder samples: p̂_θ( x | z ) lives in pixel/latent space "the video looks great" measures the SECOND thing, not the first. The invented in-between frames come from the decoder's prior, not the world model.

To separate them you must either decode the ground-truth features too and compare frame-by-frame, or evaluate in z-space where the world model actually lives. Otherwise you are crediting the world model for motion the decoder made up.

What you can claim

  • features are decodable at all → they carry visual structure
  • decoded futures are consistent with actions
  • qualitative sanity, nothing more

What you cannot claim

  • that predicted motion is accurate
  • that in-between frames are correct
  • any quantitative world-model score

08 · signals — the right frame for the video phase

20 fps → 5 Hz is decimation by 4. The decoder is bandwidth extension.

This is the answer to “can we apply signals here?”. Yes — and it makes the whole thing precise. Decimation destroys information in a way that is provable: frequencies above the new Nyquist fold back (alias), and the fold is many-to-one.

drag the true frequency — watch it alias …
a tone at 4 Hz, decimated from 20 fps by 4, reappears at 1 Hz. The decoder cannot invert this — it must choose a pre-image.
decimation by s: y[m] = x[m·s] many-to-one ⇒ not invertible new sample rate: R/s = 20/4 = 5 Hz ⇒ new Nyquist = 2.5 Hz any tone in (2.5, 10] Hz folds: f_alias = | f − round( f / (R/s) ) · (R/s) | f = 4 Hz → |4 − 5| = 1 Hz (verified numerically)

Classical interpolation (sinc / Wiener)

  • optimal for band-limited signals
  • MMSE ⇒ returns E[x | z]
  • MMSE-optimal must be the smooth average
  • video is not band-limited → blur

Diffusion decoder

  • models p(x_missing | x_kept)
  • samples the folded band from a prior
  • sharp, but not MSE-optimal
  • cost: N sequential passes

Where DSP does pay off is the solver: predictor-corrector, Heun, and multistep samplers are classical numerical integration applied to a learned score. Verified: first-order Euler on a nonlinear score has error halving as N doubles — exactly O(1/N).

09 · the maths, as functions

Score, Tweedie, and the sampler — written out

forward SDE (data → noise): dx = f(x,t) dt + g(t) dw reverse SDE (noise → data) — the only learned object is the SCORE: dx = [ f(x,t) − g(t)² ∇ₓ log p_t(x) ] dt + g(t) dw̄ probability-flow ODE (deterministic, SAME marginals): dx = [ f(x,t) − ½ g(t)² ∇ₓ log p_t(x) ] dt Tweedie — the score IS a denoiser: E[x₀ | x_t] = ( x_t + σ_t² ∇ log p_t(x_t) ) / α_t ⇒ learning the score ⇔ learning ε_θ. Verified vs exact GMM posteriors, err < 1e-8. denoising score matching loss: L(θ) = E_{x,ε,σ} ‖ ε − ε_θ(α_t x + σ_t ε, σ_t, z) ‖² classifier-free guidance (z = your V-JEPA features): ε̃ = ε_θ(x,t,∅) + w · ( ε_θ(x,t,z) − ε_θ(x,t,∅) )

Read the guidance line carefully. w is the knob that trades “faithful to the features” against “plausible motion” — which is exactly the trap in §06. Turn w up and the decoder invents confidently; the video looks better and measures the world model worse.

Noise schedules (the actual functions)

VP / DDPM linear: β(t) = β_min + t(β_max − β_min), ᾱ_t = exp( −∫₀ᵗ β(u) du ) cosine (Nichol & Dhariwal 2021): ᾱ_t = cos²( (t/T + s)/(1+s) · π/2 ) / cos²( s/(1+s) · π/2 ), s = 0.008 verified at T=1000: linear ᾱ₉₉₉ = 4.0e-05 cosine ᾱ₉₉₉ = 3.8e-33 SNR at t=500: linear = 0.084 cosine = 0.976
